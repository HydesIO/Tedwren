# 1. What changed, and why

## 1.1 Naming — new in v6.4

**The company is Tedwren Ltd.** That is settled. Contracts, IP assignments, the Companies House filing and the repository ownership are all in the company name.

**The product names are not settled.** INDUCTED was withdrawn because it described one feature of one of the two products and pointed at the wrong category. PERMITTD was proposed in v6.3 and is also not confirmed — nothing has been registered, filed, reserved or announced under it, and it should not be treated as final by anyone reading this document.

Until names are chosen, this document uses four terms and nothing else:

- **Tedwren Ltd** — the company.

- **the platform** — the shared foundation both products sit on.

- **the subcontractor product** — MVP one, time and attendance.

- **the main contractor product** — MVP two, workforce management.

**What this means for the build.** Do not put a brand name anywhere it is expensive to change. Repositories, cloud resources, database and service identifiers, environment names, queue and bucket names, email sending domains and OAuth application names should carry a neutral internal codename of Leigh’s choosing. The customer-visible name belongs in configuration and in one place in the front end, so that renaming later is a settings change and a marketing exercise rather than a migration. The rename instruction in v6.3 existed to avoid paying this cost once; it now stands twice over, because the name is not merely changing — it is undecided.

**Naming is not blocking you.** It sits on the launch critical path, not the build critical path: it is blocked on trademark clearance in classes 9, 41 and 42, on domain and social handle availability, and on app store name reservation. Nothing in Section 5 waits on it.

## 1.2 What changed from v5.0

v5.0 defined two products sharing one platform and sequenced the shared foundation first. That structure holds. Three things have changed since, and they change scope rather than direction.

- The two products are now each a defined MVP with a hard scope boundary, rather than a single sequence with an implied cut line. Everything outside those two boundaries is a numbered later phase.

- Competitive analysis of the UK incumbents — principally Chime Software, and behind it Donseed, Biosite, MSite, Re-flow and Datascope — identified capability we had not accounted for. The material ones are folded into the MVPs; the rest are phased.

- Biometric verification moves from a permanent exclusion to a later phase. It is the single strongest claim the incumbents make and we have no answer to it. It is still emphatically not MVP.

- **New in v6.4:** the sign-in model no longer assumes a site has a compound. Retrofit and dispersed-property schemes have no gate, no cabin and nothing we are allowed to attach a code to. That was a hole in the requirements and it is also a differentiator — see SF-25, SF-26, MC-28 and Section 3.4.

# 2. Context

We started with digital induction and sign-in software sold to principal contractors. A strategic review of the UK market found the defensible category is not induction software but workforce compliance — a system that decides whether a worker may start, and proves it afterwards. A working session on 27 July found that the same underlying data serves two different buyers with two different problems. So: one platform, two products, sold separately.

|                         | **Subcontractor product**                                                                                                                       | **Main contractor product**                                                                                                        |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Category name in market | Time and attendance                                                                                                                             | Workforce management                                                                                                               |
| The promise             | Keep every operative site-ready, know exactly who worked where and for how long, and send a compliance pack to any contractor in seconds.       | Know who is on site, whether they are compliant, and why anyone has been blocked.                                                  |
| Buyer                   | Specialist subcontractor, roughly 10–100 operatives, working across several clients’ sites                                                      | Principal or medium regional contractor running its own sites, roughly 5–25 sites                                                  |
| Daily user              | The operative, confirming arrival and departure from their own phone every day. Behind them, the compliance or payroll administrator at a desk. | The operative, signing in and out from their own phone every day. Behind them, the site manager on a phone or a laptop in a cabin. |
| Sale                    | Easier and faster. No external dependencies. Our first revenue.                                                                                 | Higher value, slower cycle.                                                                                                        |
| Priced by               | Operative headcount band                                                                                                                        | Active site                                                                                                                        |

The insight underneath both products is the one to build for: subcontractors repeatedly prove the same people, cards and competencies to different contractors, while contractors repeatedly rebuild and recheck the same information. Both sides pay for identical duplicated work. Owning both sides of that exchange is the long-term position. We are not building all of it now, but the architecture should not rule it out.

## 3.3 What their feature list tells us about their business

Stated here because it shapes where we compete. Of roughly 150 published features, close to half concern timesheets, pay rules, overtime, leave, expenses and payroll reporting. Their health and safety capability is a document distribution and signature engine with a checklist builder attached. It is good, and it is not a decision engine — nothing in it programmatically refuses entry against live compliance state and then reconstructs that refusal afterwards.

So the honest reading is that they are a payroll and labour cost product that has grown compliance features, and we are a compliance product that needs to grow pay features. That asymmetry is why Phase 4 exists, why we should not fight them on verified hours before Phase 5, and why the compliance pack and the defensible entry decision are where the argument is winnable.

## 3.4 Where we are actually different

Five things. Everything in Sections 5 to 7 exists to protect one of them.

- No app install for workers. Every incumbent named above ships a native app; several require a kiosk or a fixed reader. A worker at a gate will not install anything. This is rule R1 and it is the largest single adoption advantage we have.

- The compliance pack. Nobody in the field lets a subcontractor assemble evidence and send it to a recipient with no account and no relationship with us. It is the subcontractor product’s reason to exist and our cheapest route to main contractor customers.

- A site-entry decision that explains itself. The incumbents record attendance and distribute documents. They do not programmatically refuse entry against live compliance state and then reconstruct that decision eighteen months later. That reconstructability is rule R10 and it is what a main contractor is buying.

- Worker-permissioned sharing between two customers. Phase 6, and the reason both products share one data foundation.

- Sign-in on schemes with no compound. Every incumbent assumes a site with a gate, a cabin, a kiosk or a fixed reader. Retrofit, planned maintenance, social housing and dispersed-property work has none of those, and it is a large and publicly funded segment. A sign-in model that needs no physical point of presence is the one place where we are not merely cheaper than the incumbents but the only credible option. See SF-25 and MC-28. New in v6.4, and it is now five things rather than four.

| *And one place we are deliberately weaker, stated so nobody is surprised by it in a sales meeting: we have no answer to buddy-punching until Phase 5. Verified attendance is the incumbents’ strongest pitch and for a buyer whose pain is hours fraud rather than compliance evidence, they are right and we are not competitive. Qualify that buyer out until Phase 5 ships.* |

**The problem SF-25 and SF-26 exist to solve, in the words it was put to us:**

| *“Some main contractors won’t have a site compound. For example on retrofit projects, they may just be replacing windows in a property. So the trades will just go straight to the property and start work. So a QR code on a site premises won’t work for this as we can’t stick a QR code on the door of every occupied property. There needs to be a workaround for this scenario, this is also a good USP for retrofit projects who work on dispersed properties.”* |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

Three consequences worth stating plainly, because each one constrains the design:

- **Sign-in cannot depend on anything physically present at the location.** Not a printed code, not a sticker, not a tablet in a cabin, not a device someone has to remember to bring. If the mechanism requires an object to exist at the property, it does not work here.

- **The property is occupied and the occupier is not our user.** A resident’s front door is not a surface we may attach anything to, and a resident cannot be asked to hold, display or operate anything on our behalf. This rules out several answers that look reasonable at first.

- **A scheme of two hundred properties is one site, not two hundred.** That is a commercial statement as much as a technical one — see Section 9. Billing per property would make the product unsellable into precisely the segment where we have no competition.

**Accepted when:**

- Adding someone already known to the platform under a different company creates a second engagement record, not a second person, and reveals nothing about the other company.

- Adding someone twice within the same company is refused, naming the existing record.

- Marking a leaver removes them from the register and the billing count while leaving their attendance, cards and history fully searchable.

- An uncertain card reading produces a card marked for review, and that card does not appear as verified anywhere — including in a compliance pack.

- A worker with a card expiring in 30 days receives a text and their administrator receives an email, and running the check twice in a day does not send it twice.

- If the expiry check fails to run, someone is told. Nothing about the interface would otherwise reveal that the data has gone stale, which makes this the most dangerous silent failure in the product.

- On a scheme with no compound, a worker standing at property 14 confirms arrival from their own phone with nothing printed, installed or attached at that property — and the record produced carries the same status, and appears the same in a timesheet and in an audit export, as one produced by scanning a code at a gate.

- An administrator adds a further property to an existing scheme without visiting it and without anything being sent to it.

| *One thing to get right early: mobile numbers arrive in every conceivable format, and the same person will be entered as “Joe Smith” by one company and “Joseph Smith” by another. How numbers are normalised needs to be settled once and applied everywhere, because getting it wrong quietly creates duplicate people and undermines everything in Phase 6. Names should not be reconciled at all — each company keeps the name it recorded, and that is the name that appears on its documents.* |

## 8.1 Phase 1 — Live CSCS verification

- A card is checked against CSCS Smart Check in real time. An expired card blocks the induction. An unrecognised card lets the induction continue but blocks entry until a manager checks it manually.

- If CSCS is unreachable, the product falls back to reading the card and having a person check it — the same as the base product. An unavailable third-party service must never stop a worker completing an induction, because the alternative is a site full of people who cannot start.

- A card verified live is labelled differently from a card checked by the customer themselves. Without this module, no call to CSCS is made and the product describes cards as checked by the customer, not verified by CSCS.

- The same card number registered against two different people is flagged as a possible problem. Flag both, name neither — blocking would let one customer’s typo lock a real worker out of an unrelated site.

| *Worth using openly in the sales conversation rather than hiding: reading a card tells you what it says; live verification tells you whether it is real and still valid. A photograph of a cancelled card reads perfectly. Most subcontractors know their own people and are content with the first. Main contractors admitting strangers to a site increasingly are not.* |

## 8.4 Phase 4 — Pay and cost

- A pay rules engine applied to approved hours: standard and overtime rates, rounding, break deduction, minimum call-out, travel time, night and weekend premiums, bank holidays, and per-worker or per-trade rate cards.

- Leave and absence requested by the worker from their phone, approved by a manager, reflected in the timesheet.

- Labour cost by site, by trade and by cost code, with productive and non-productive time distinguished.

- Export to payroll and accounting — Sage, Xero and a generic format — plus CIS handling and self-bill support for the subcontractor side.

- Rounding rules and time bands applied automatically to each timesheet, configurable by the customer rather than by us. Overtime rates applied by time band so that a rate is set once and calculated thereafter without intervention.

- Customer-defined leave types — annual, sick, paid, unpaid, compassionate — producing a single payroll report rather than several.

- A business closure window set once and applied across the whole workforce, drawing down leave allowance, with an exception list. Small feature, disproportionate goodwill.

- Expense claims submitted from the phone against a configurable approval workflow.

- Output-based pay, where cost is tied to completed and verified work rather than hours present. This is the natural bridge to Phase 3 and should be understood before the pay engine is designed, not bolted on afterwards.

- Leave and cover planned by site and by skill, so a gap is visible before it becomes a delay.

- Every figure remains traceable to the underlying sign-in records under rule R16.

## 8.5 Phase 5 — Verified identity and attendance integrity

This answers the incumbents’ strongest claim. It is also the phase with the most ways to go wrong, so the conditions are set out here rather than left to the build.

- Face capture at enrolment and face match at sign-in, with liveness detection, and optional device binding. Geofencing is no longer part of this phase — it has moved into both MVPs at SF-14, which means this phase adds identity assurance on top of location assurance rather than both at once.

- A people map: where operatives are and have been, filterable by site, person and trade, built on the location data SF-14 already produces.

- A DPIA is completed and documented before any live enrolment. Biometric data used to identify a person is special-category data under UK GDPR and the ICO expects a demonstrated necessity and proportionality argument, not an assertion of one.

- A non-biometric alternative is permanently available to every worker on every site, offered without penalty and without making the worker ask for it. This is rule R17 and it is not negotiable.

- Biometric templates are stored as irreversible representations, never as images, in the UK, with a defined retention period tied to engagement rather than indefinitely.

- The module is switchable per customer and per site, and its absence is stated rather than merely absent.

| *The unresolved tension, flagged early because it may change your architecture. Rule R1 says no app install. Every incumbent does face match through a native app or a fixed kiosk, and browser-based capture has materially weaker liveness detection than a native SDK. We are not willing to trade R1 for this. Three routes exist — browser capture with accepted weaker assurance, an optional app for customers who want verified attendance while the browser path remains for everyone else, or a site-supplied kiosk device. We have a preference for the second and would like your view on it before Phase 2 starts, because the answer may affect how worker identity is modelled from the beginning.* |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

## 8.6 Phase 6 — Cross-company sharing and the worker record

- Nothing crosses between companies unless the worker has agreed to it. There is no administrative override and no company-level switch. A company cannot give permission on a worker’s behalf.

- Permission is specific: which company may see it, what category it covers — induction status, attendance, card status — and for which site.

- Shared information is read-only, reduced, and credited. The receiving company sees that an induction is held, where, and until when. Not the quiz score, not the signature, not the card images.

- A worker can see every permission they have given, from their phone, and withdraw any of them immediately without giving a reason. Withdrawal stops future sharing; it does not recall anything already sent, and the worker is told this in plain words at the moment they withdraw.

- Where no permission exists, the employer is told that evidence exists elsewhere without being shown it, and can ask the worker to share it. This one needs a legal decision — see Section 10.3.

- Where a subcontractor’s recorded site is in fact a site run by a main contractor on the platform, the two can be connected. A connection is always suggested and then confirmed by a person, never made automatically, and confirming it alone grants no access — the worker’s permission is still required.

## 8.7 Phase 7 — Integrations and the network

- Inbound events from third-party access hardware a customer already owns.

- A documented API and webhooks for customers with their own systems.

- Named integrations, in the order the market will ask for them: Procore, Sage, Xero, and the CHAS client portal for supply chain management.

- A library of pre-built reports accumulated across the customer base. Worth naming as a deliberate asset because it compounds and cannot be shortcut: it is built from customers, not from code, and it is one of the things a nine-year-old competitor has that we cannot buy.

- Pull-based standing access between a subcontractor and a main contractor.

- Evidence packs for insurance brokers and underwriters at renewal — induction completion, qualification currency, acknowledgement rates, incident evidence, auditable presence.

**Modules must be genuinely switchable. Three things need to be true, however you implement it:**

- Turning a module off stops the capability and hides it, but never deletes what was created while it was on. A customer who lapses and returns should find their history intact.

- Where a module changes what the site-entry decision checks, the customer is told plainly. If RAMS is not being enforced at their gate, that must be stated where they will see it, not merely absent.

- A customer never sees a locked door advertising something they have not bought.

**How we talk about it**

The sales message is time saved, fewer blocked workers, faster site starts, cards that do not silently expire, and proof available immediately. Regulation reinforces the urgency; it is not the headline. In particular the Building Safety Act’s record-keeping duties apply principally to higher-risk buildings in England rather than every project, and overstating that will be caught by any informed buyer. The product should support those duties well and claim them modestly.

## 10.3 Legal

Ours to resolve, flagged here because it touches design and because it is on the critical path rather than after it. We are starting this review in parallel with the early build, not after it.

- Who is legally responsible for the shared worker record, and for each customer’s data about that worker.

- Whether the worker’s agreement is the right basis for sharing between companies, and what wording makes it genuinely voluntary.

- What happens when a worker asks to be erased, given that some records carry a retention obligation — and given that attendance records feeding payroll carry a statutory one.

- What basis a subcontractor relies on when disclosing worker details to a client in a compliance pack.

- Whether telling an employer that unshared evidence exists is itself an acceptable disclosure.

- The basis for recording a worker’s location at sign-in and sign-out under SF-14, what is retained, for how long, and what a worker is told about it. Location is personal data and geofencing has moved into the MVP, so this is now a first-release question rather than a Phase 5 one.

- The DPIA for Phase 5, which should be scoped now even though the phase is distant, because its conclusions may constrain how identity is modelled from the beginning.

- Recording a worker’s location at a private residential property under SF-25 and SF-26. This is a materially different question from a geofence around a construction site: the boundary sits over somebody’s home, the occupier is not a party to anything, and the record shows a named individual at a named residential address on a named date. Retention and precision both need a defensible answer before the first retrofit pilot.

# 12. Glossary

| **Term**                    | **Meaning**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Operative                   | A trade worker who physically works on site — electrician, plumber, scaffolder.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Principal / main contractor | The company responsible for overall site management and health and safety. Runs the site, approves RAMS, controls who gets in.                                                                                                                     |
| Subcontractor               | A company engaged to carry out a specific scope of work. Sends its operatives to other companies’ sites.                                                                                                                                           |
| CSCS                        | Construction Skills Certification Scheme. The UK industry standard for proving construction competence. Not a legal requirement, but most large contractors require a card.                                                                        |
| CSCS Smart Check            | CSCS’s own service for verifying a card is genuine and current. Available as an API. Underpins Phase 1.                                                                                                                                            |
| RAMS                        | Risk Assessment and Method Statement. A document a subcontractor must submit and have approved before its people start work.                                                                                                                       |
| Induction                   | The mandatory site safety briefing every operative completes before working on a particular site. Site-specific, and the site operator’s legal duty.                                                                                               |
| Toolbox talk                | A short, recurring safety briefing given by an employer rather than a site operator. Different duty, different purpose. Phase 2.                                                                                                                   |
| CDM 2015                    | Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015. Sets the duties around induction, competence and RAMS that the main contractor product automates.                                                                                           |
| Golden thread               | A Building Safety Act 2022 duty to keep a digital record of who did what work and when, applying principally to higher-risk buildings in England. We support it; we do not lead with it.                                                           |
| Site-ready                  | Our term. A worker whose required cards are current and whose evidence is complete, by their employer’s own standard. Distinct from being permitted onto a specific site, which only that site’s operator can decide.                              |
| Compliance pack             | A bundle of worker and company compliance evidence a subcontractor sends to a client. Fixed at the moment of sending. The subcontractor product’s defining feature.                                                                                |
| Competency cover            | A requirement that a named competency — first aider, fire marshal, appointed person — is physically present on a site while work is in progress. Distinct from tracking whose card expires when.                                                   |
| DPIA                        | Data Protection Impact Assessment. Required before biometric processing begins. See rule R17.                                                                                                                                                      |
| Dispersed scheme            | A project made up of many individual properties rather than one enclosed site — retrofit, planned maintenance, social housing works. No compound, no gate, no single boundary. One site commercially; many boundaries technically.                 |
| Retrofit                    | Upgrading an existing, usually occupied, building — insulation, windows, heating, ventilation. Publicly funded at scale in the UK and almost always delivered across dispersed occupied properties, which is why it drives SF-25, SF-26 and MC-28. |
